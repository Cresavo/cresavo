**What actually preserves the option.** The legal requirement is sufficient rights in the code you want to offer under different terms. You can dual-license code you control. For outside contributions received solely under AGPL, you need another valid permission from the relevant rightsholders, such as a CLA, assignment, or later written grant, or you need to replace that code. A DCO certifies the right to submit under the indicated licence. With neither a CLA nor a DCO, GitHub’s terms generally give contributions the repository’s licence. Previously released AGPL copies retain their AGPL permissions while recipients comply. Third-party dependencies keep their own terms. You can already charge for hosting, support, or AGPL copies subject to the licence. The separate right you’re preserving is the right to offer the same core on **non-AGPL terms**. [GNU AGPLv3, especially §§2, 4–6, 10 and 13](https://opensource.org/license/agpl-v3), [GitHub Terms §D.6](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service#6-contributions-under-repository-license), [DCO 1.1](https://developercertificate.org/), [Grafana’s commercial licensing example](https://grafana.com/blog/qa-with-our-ceo-on-relicensing/)

## Recommended setup for Cresavo

1. **Keep the public core `AGPL-3.0-only`.** Publish an explicit, non-exclusive CLA before merging outside code. Record acceptance manually at first. Use free hosted CLA Assistant when the volume justifies it. [pretix CLA rationale](https://pretix.eu/about/en/blog/20210412-license/), [CLA Assistant](https://github.com/cla-assistant/cla-assistant)
